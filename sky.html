    Cosminova — the sky from where you stand

    Looking around

    Drag to turn your head; scroll or pinch to zoom, from a 110° naked-eye view down to seven arcseconds — fifty thousand times, enough to put three kilometres of lunar surface across the screen.

    Click anything to lock onto it. The view then holds it centred against the sky's rotation, so you can keep scrolling in until a planet becomes a world. Search by proper name, Bayer letter, Messier or NGC number, constellation or planet.

    What you are looking at

    This is the real sky for the date, time and place in the options panel. Star positions and magnitudes come from the HYG catalogue; the Sun, Moon and planets are computed from Keplerian elements with the ELP2000 lunar series, so the Moon shows the correct phase and libration.

    In ground view the sky is rotated into your local horizon frame, the atmosphere scatters sunlight into a physically shaped twilight, and the visibility limit tracks sky brightness — moonlight and light pollution wash out faint stars the way they do outdoors. Switch to free space to lift the horizon away and see the whole celestial sphere.

    Star catalogue: HYG v4.1 (Hipparcos, Yale BSC, Gliese). Deep sky: OpenNGC. Milky Way panorama: ESO/S. Brunier. Planet imagery: Solar System Scope (CC BY 4.0), NASA/JPL.
